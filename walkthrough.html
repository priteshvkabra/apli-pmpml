Your bus ticket, one tap before the conductor reaches youतुमचं रोजचं तिकीट, एका टॅपवर

A working prototype for Apli PMPML, Pune's city bus app, in its own screens and colours. Riders who have the app still buy from the conductor, because the conductor does the work: they say one stop name and the conductor makes the ticket. In the app, they make it themselves, from a blank form, in English, often for the first time in a packed bus. These five changes, in order of priority, make the app do that work.

Never ridden a Pune bus? Read this first

PMPML runs Pune's city buses. On board, a conductor sells paper tickets from a handheld machine. You pay cash, or he shows a UPI QR and you pay from Google Pay, PhonePe or any UPI app. That QR only takes the money; the ticket is still paper.

You can also buy in the Apli PMPML app and show the ticket on your phone. A ticket is for one bus route number. The conductor checks the app ticket by eye: route, stops, fare, time.

PMPML wants more riders to book in the app, so conductors sell less and spend more time checking tickets and keeping passengers safe.

You can also tap the phone directly. The language button at the top of Home switches the whole app.
